FOR FIRST-TIMERS

Best Himalayan Treks for Beginners

You don't need prior trekking experience to fall in love with the mountains. Here's how to pick your first trip.

What Actually Matters for a First Trek

New trekkers often worry about the wrong things. Distance in kilometres matters less than three other factors:

  • Altitude — treks that stay under 3,000 m are generally easier on the body and carry less risk of altitude sickness than those pushing past 4,000 m.
  • Duration — a 2–3 day trip is a gentler introduction than a week-long expedition, both physically and in terms of time away from routine.
  • Terrain — well-marked trails through forest and meadow are far friendlier than scree slopes or exposed ridge walks.

Fitness: What You Actually Need

You don't need to be an athlete. If you can comfortably climb 4–5 flights of stairs without stopping, or walk briskly for 45 minutes, you have a reasonable base for any of the beginner-friendly treks below. A couple of weeks of daily walking or light cardio before your trip will make the experience noticeably more enjoyable.

Our Easiest Departures to Start With

Chakrata — A Weekend Introduction

At just 2 days and 1 night, Chakrata is the shortest commitment on our calendar, and the terrain is gentle throughout. It's an ideal way to test whether trekking and group travel suits you, without a long trip away from home.

Jibhi & Tirthan Valley — Easy Trails, Big Reward

Jibhi's waterfalls and the walk to Serolsar Lake involve well-defined trails through forest, with no technical sections. It's a favourite for travellers who want scenery without a demanding climb.

McLeodganj – Triund — A Gentle Summit

The trek to Triund is graded moderate, but it's a single well-trodden trail with tea stalls along the way, making it a confidence-building first "real" trek for many first-timers, with a genuine summit feeling at the top.

What to Pack, Briefly

  • Sturdy shoes with grip — trail runners or trekking shoes, broken in before the trip
  • Layers — a t-shirt, a fleece or light jacket, and a windproof/waterproof outer layer
  • A reusable water bottle and some energy snacks for the trail
  • A small daypack, sunscreen, and a torch or headlamp
